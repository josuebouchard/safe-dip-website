## Mechanical Structure

The frame is assembled from 4040 T-slot aluminum extrusion, forming the horizontal beam, vertical column, and supporting legs. The T-slot profile allows components to be mounted at arbitrary positions along each beam and secured with T-nuts and M5 hardware, simplifying alignment and adjustment of the input, dipping, and output station positions.

The horizontal axis is guided by an MGN12H linear rail and carriage, driven by a GT2 timing belt coupled to a NEMA 23 stepper. The vertical axis uses a TR8x2 leadscrew supported by pillow-block bearings and coupled to a NEMA 23 stepper through a flexible shaft coupler — the leadscrew is mechanically self-locking, so the carriage holds its position without active braking during a power loss or fault. An anti-backlash nut block removes axial play so programmed immersion depth stays repeatable across cycles.

## Leadscrew Payload Calculations

The vertical-axis capacity is determined by combining the leadscrew kinematics with the motor's torque–speed characteristic. For a leadscrew with lead $L$, the required rotational speed follows from the desired linear speed:

$$
v = n L
$$

The motor's torque–speed curve then gives the available torque $T(\omega)$ at that operating speed, which converts into the maximum safe payload:

$$
F_\text{axial} = \frac{2 \pi T(\omega)\, \eta}{L}
$$

$$
m_\text{max} = \frac{F_\text{axial}}{g} = \frac{2 \pi T(\omega)\, \eta}{L g}
$$

$$
m_\text{safe} = \frac{m_\text{max}}{S} = \frac{2 \pi T(\omega)\, \eta}{S L g}
$$

Here $L$ is the leadscrew lead — the axial distance the nut travels in one complete revolution. $\eta$ is the mechanical efficiency of the leadscrew, accounting for frictional losses, and $S$ is the safety factor. This design applies $S = 2$ to account for uncertainties in loading, acceleration, drivetrain losses not captured by the nominal efficiency, and variation between theoretical and available motor torque.

The torque used above must be taken from the motor's torque–speed curve at the intended operating speed. Static holding torque must **not** be used for a moving-axis calculation, because available motor torque decreases as rotational speed increases.
